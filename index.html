The sale you never knew you lost

Someone finds the thing they want on your site, gets to the checkout, sees two to three days, and needs it sooner. They close the tab. Nothing about that shows up in your figures, so it never gets fixed.

Every other delivery option asks the customer to do a calculation. Two to three days from when? Does that include the weekend? Will it come before Friday? Same-day is the one option where the answer needs no working out, because the answer is today.

Same-day does not add a service to your checkout. It removes a reason to leave it.

What it costs

€9.95 + VATper delivery, a bag €12.95 for a suitcase
  • Nothing to join. No setup fee, no monthly charge, no minimum.
  • No commission, ever. The same fee on a €30 candle as on a €600 coat. Nothing grows with the basket.
  • One price for a bag, wherever it goes. €9.95 whether it crosses the street or crosses the M50. A suitcase is €12.95 because it takes more room and a tighter area, and the booking screen shows which one applies before anyone commits to it.
  • You set what the customer pays. Pass the fee on and it costs you nothing, carry part of it to lift take-up, or charge above it and earn on every delivery.
  • One invoice a month. Not a card charge on every parcel.
  • Stop whenever it suits. No contract.

Every order between one collection and the next

The window is not the morning. It runs from one 2pm to the next, so an order at four yesterday afternoon and an order at three this morning are on the same run. Nothing that arrives while you are shut has to wait for a second day.

Midnight is the only thing that changes, and it changes what the customer is told rather than when the parcel moves. Order at nine in the evening and the checkout says tomorrow. Order at nine the next morning and it says today. Both go out on the same run.

Which makes the orders sitting in your inbox when you arrive the easiest ones you will send. They have been on the list since before you got in, and you have the whole morning to pack them.

What we take, and how far

Two sizes, and the reach differs between them. Nothing here needs a conversation with us: every booking starts with the customer’s Eircode, and the check tells your staff which sizes are available for that address before they type anything else.

A bag

Up to 3 kg

Broadly anywhere inside the M50

Anything that fits in a shopping bag. Most of what a shop sends is this, and it reaches the widest area from any shop on hop.

  • Clothing, lingerie, shoes
  • Jewellery and watches
  • Prescriptions and pharmacy items
  • Books, cosmetics, small homeware
  • A bottle of wine
A suitcase

Up to 20 kg

City centre shops, broadly inside the canals

Anything one person can lift. If your shop is in the city centre and the customer is inside the canals, it is offered automatically alongside the bag.

  • A case of wine
  • Boxed kitchenware, a set of glasses
  • Several bags in one order
  • Bedding, larger homeware

Those two boundaries are the honest shape of it rather than a hard line on a map, because some addresses just outside work and some just inside do not. The Eircode settles it either way, and an address we cannot reach never sees a same-day option at all, so nobody is offered a delivery that cannot happen.

A bag is €9.95 plus VAT and a suitcase is €12.95, whatever is inside it. Neither figure moves with the value of the order.
