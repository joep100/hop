Two ways it goes on your checkout

One you can switch on yourself next week, and a proper version that replaces it once you know your customers want it. They look much the same to a shopper. What happens behind them is not the same thing at all.

The difference, without the jargon

Start here

A sign in the window

You set up a local delivery area in your own Shopify settings. Anyone inside it is offered the option, at any hour of the day, whether or not it happens to be true for them at that moment.

Three minutes and it costs nothing. It knows where somebody lives. It does not know what time it is.

Then this

Someone who checks

The customer types their address. Before your checkout draws the delivery options it asks us a question: can you get to this address today? We look at the Eircode, look at the clock, and answer. Only then does the option appear.

Every checkout, about a second, and the customer never sees it happen. If the answer is no they simply never see same-day offered.

The first tells everyone the rule. The second answers every case.

Version one: switch it on yourself

Use Local delivery rather than a shipping rate. It is a separate feature in Shopify, built for exactly this, and the important difference is that it lets you draw the area properly instead of settling for the whole county.

Why this beats a shipping zone

Shopify’s shipping zones cannot go below county level. A County Dublin zone would offer same-day to Balbriggan, thirty kilometres north of the M50. A radius does not.

4

Noon, not 2pm, and here is why

A rider calls to you at 2pm. If you tell customers 2pm, an order landing at ten to two has not been packed and cannot go. Telling them noon gives you two hours to pack, which is the whole point of the gap.

Noon is a starting suggestion rather than a rule. If you can turn an order round in ten minutes, tell them half one. If you batch them mid-morning, tell them eleven. It is your number and you can change it in a minute.

What version one still cannot do

It cannot tell the time. Someone ordering at five in the afternoon sees the option, which is why the name has to say what actually happens. And it draws a circle, so a handful of addresses inside the radius will be ones we cannot reach on a given day.

Neither matters much for a month, because your staff see every order before anything moves and the volumes are small. What you are finding out is whether your customers want same-day at all, and that question does not need a perfect gate to answer.

Version two: the checkout asks us

This is the one the rest of the site describes. It needs a small app on your store, which we build and you install in a couple of clicks. After that you never touch it.

1

The customer types their address

Ordinary checkout, nothing unusual. They put in 42 Waterloo Road, Dublin 4.

2

Your checkout asks us a question

Before it draws the delivery options, Shopify sends us the address and waits. The customer sees a spinner for about a second.

3

We check and answer

Is that exact Eircode on a route. Is it before your cut-off. Is your shop open today. Then we reply with your price, or with nothing at all.

4

The option appears, or it does not

Same-day at your price, dated today or tomorrow depending on the hour. Or absent, and the customer sees your usual options and nothing else.

What changes for you

Nothing at the counter and nothing in your admin. The local delivery option you set up by hand comes out, the app goes in, and from then on it looks after itself. You still set the price and you can still change it whenever you like.

The reason to wait is not the work. It is that version one answers the only question worth asking first, and if the answer is no you have lost three minutes rather than a fortnight.

Then the part that is ours

  • Tell us it is on. We add you to the collection round and give you a booking link for the counter.
  • When an order comes in with that option chosen, book it through the link. Eircode, name, mobile, done.
  • A rider calls at 2pm. Whatever is packed goes out.
  • After a month, look at the numbers. How many chose it, what they spent, whether any of them came back.

Shopify moves things around in its admin from time to time, so a menu may sit in a slightly different place than shown here. The five steps are the same.
