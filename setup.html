Two ways it goes on your checkout

There is a version you can switch on yourself next week, and a proper version that replaces it once you know your customers want it. They look identical to a shopper. What happens behind them is completely different.

The difference, without the jargon

Version one

A sign in the window

You put up a notice that says same-day delivery is available in Dublin. It says the same thing to everybody, all day, whether or not it happens to be true for the person reading it.

You set it up in three minutes and it costs nothing. It cannot tell a Rathfarnham address from a Ranelagh one, and it does not know what time it is.

Version two

Someone at the counter who checks

The customer types their address. Before your checkout draws the delivery options, it asks us a question: can you get to this address today? We look at the Eircode, look at the clock, and answer yes or no. Only then does the option appear.

That happens on every single checkout, in about a second, and the customer never sees it happen. If the answer is no, they simply never see same-day offered.

Version one tells everyone the rule. Version two answers every case.

Version one: switch it on yourself

No app to install and nothing for us to build. You add a shipping rate the same way you would add any other, sitting inside a Dublin-only zone.

What version one does not do

A rate you add yourself covers the whole of County Dublin, so it will show for a few addresses we cannot reach and it does not know what time it is. For the first month that is usually fine, because you are finding out whether your customers want same-day at all, and your staff see every order before it goes anywhere.

The app version replaces it later. That one checks the exact Eircode and the clock, so the option only appears when it can actually be met. Same rate at the checkout, better decision behind it.

Version two: the checkout asks us

This is the one the site describes. It needs a small app on your store, which we build and you install in a couple of clicks. After that you never touch it.

1

The customer types their address

Ordinary checkout, nothing unusual. They put in 42 Waterloo Road, Dublin 4.

2

Your checkout asks us a question

Before it draws the delivery options, Shopify sends us the address and waits. The customer sees a spinner for about a second.

3

We check three things and answer

Is that Eircode on a route. Is it before your cut-off. Is your shop switched on today. Then we reply with your price, or with nothing at all.

4

The option appears, or it does not

Same-day sits at the top of the list at your price, dated today or tomorrow. Or it is absent, and the customer sees your usual options and nothing else.

What changes for you

Nothing at the counter and nothing in your admin. The rate you added by hand comes out, the app goes in, and from then on the option looks after itself. You still set the price, and you can change it whenever you like.

The reason to wait is not the work. It is that version one answers the only question worth asking first, which is whether your customers choose same-day at all. If they do, this is worth building. If they do not, you have lost three minutes.

Then the part that is ours

  • Tell us it is on. We add your shop to the collection round and give you a booking link for the counter.
  • When an order comes in with that rate chosen, book it through the link. Eircode, name, mobile, and it is done.
  • A rider calls at 2pm. Whatever is packed goes out.
  • After a month, look at the numbers. How many chose it, what they spent, whether any of them came back. That is the only evidence worth having.

Shopify moves things around in its admin from time to time, so a menu may sit in a slightly different place than shown here. The five steps are the same.
