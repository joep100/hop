You have done this before

If you offer Klarna, you already accept that a third party’s badge sits on your product page and carries a promise on your behalf, because it sells more. hop is the same arrangement for the other half of the shopper’s question. One badge answers how they pay. This one answers when they get it.

Cut-off times, exactly

This is the part we will not be vague about, because the badge is worthless the first time it is wrong.

  • City centre shops: 2pm. Everything ordered that morning is packed and waiting for collection at 2pm. Not being packed at 2pm. Waiting.
  • Anything ordered after 2pm goes next working day, guaranteed. So a shopper who misses same-day still beats the three days they get today.
  • Outside the city centre, we agree a collection time with you. It depends where you are and what else is on that route, so we will not put a number on it here and then break it. Talk to us and we will tell you what we can actually do for your address.

What we need from you

  • Stock in the shop. Not in a hub, not with a third-party warehouse. If you cannot put your hands on it, the badge cannot promise it.
  • Packed and waiting at the collection time. Not packing when the rider arrives. Ready.
  • Honest stock levels online. The badge appears against items you say you have. If that data is wrong, the badge lies, and it stops being worth anything to any of us.

What you get

  • The badge on your product pages, your checkout and your window.
  • A rider collecting from your door, from a courier operation that has run Dublin since 1988.
  • Tracking texts sent to your customer in your shop’s name.
  • One invoice monthly, not a card charge per parcel.

The one condition

We can take the badge off your site. If same-day stops actually being same-day, the badge has to go, because its value to you depends on shoppers trusting it everywhere they see it. That protects your investment as much as ours.

Three ways to price it, and they are all yours to choose

We charge you the same either way. What your customer sees is entirely your call, and shops take genuinely different approaches to it.

Option one

Pass it straight on

Charge your customer roughly what we charge you. Same-day pays for itself and does not touch your margin either way.

Simplest place to start, and where most shops begin.

Option two

Absorb some of it

It costs you the gap and buys you far more uptake. Free over a threshold also turns delivery into a reason to add one more item.

Option three

Make money on it

Charge above our fee, within the €19.95 ceiling, and same-day becomes a small margin line of its own.

Margin on every delivery

The price is a dial on demand, not just on margin

This is the part worth thinking about for more than a minute. What you charge decides how many customers choose same-day at all. Price it at the ceiling and a handful will take it. Make it free over €150 and a lot of people will, and every one of them is a sale that might otherwise have gone to a shop that ships tomorrow.

So it is worth moving rather than setting once. Start where you are comfortable, watch what people actually choose, and change it.

Why there is a ceiling at all

We recommend around €15 and shops showing the badge agree not to charge more than €19.95. Being straight about our interest here: the badge only becomes worth having when shoppers recognise it, and that needs volume, so we would rather you priced it keenly. But the ceiling exists for your sake too. A badge that sometimes means €40 teaches shoppers to stop looking for it, and then it is worth nothing to the shop next door either.
